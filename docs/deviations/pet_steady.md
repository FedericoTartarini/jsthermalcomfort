## Notes

- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `_pet_steady_vectorised`: upstream's two `optimize.fsolve` calls (`models/pet_steady.py:475, :480`) are the port of scipy 1.18.1's `fsolve` (`src/_internal/fsolve.ts`, deviation _shared-87, decision 280), which replaces the earlier hand-written Newton solver. `ml-levenberg-marquardt` 5.1.0 failed decision 82 on 2,003 cases measured on 4.7.0: 6 to 11 of the 1,972 cases fsolve solves rounded differently, up to 38% relative, and it threw "Matrix is not symmetric" on 2 cases where upstream returns a value.
- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `_pet_steady_vectorised`: upstream's `@np.vectorize` has no counterpart, since the model takes scalars (ADR 0019, whitelist items 8 and 9), and its nested functions `vasomotricity`, `sweat_rate`, `solve_pet` and `pet_fc` stay nested, closing over the same inputs. `solve_pet`'s two overloads type its two returns, the balance list (`list(e_bal_vec)`, a copy) and the scalar balance; the PET solve passes `tx[0]` where upstream passes the length-1 array `tx`, whose arithmetic is element-wise. `met` is reassigned as upstream reassigns it. `dict` lookups are object properties, and `max` and `min` are `Math.max` and `Math.min` (item 9).
- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `_pet_steady_vectorised`: upstream's `**` and `pow` on Python floats and numpy scalars are the C library's `pow`, so `**` and `Math.pow`, and `np.log` and `p_sat`'s `np.exp` and `np.log` are the C library's, where `Math.log` and `Math.exp` are V8's (ADR 0009). Measured in the parity image against node v26.8.2: on 100,000 values over each site's range, `Math.pow` equals glibc's `pow` for each exponent the model uses on 99,810 to 99,849, and for `x ** 4.0` from 200 to 400 better than `x * x * x * x` (65,519) or `(x * x) * (x * x)` (49,655). The constants `2.42 * 10**6`, `5.67 * 10**-8`, `10.0 ** (-6.0)` and `16.7 * 10 ** (-1)` equal upstream's bit for bit. On 2,003 measured cases the solved core, skin and clothing temperatures are bit-identical on 1,302 of the 1,988 that return, and the unrounded PET within 2.3e-10 relative.
- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `pet_fc`: upstream's `round(optimize.fsolve(f, pet_guess)[0], 2)` rounds an `np.float64`, whose `__round__` is numpy's rounding, so the library's `round` (decision 284; measured on 4.7.0: `round(np.float64(2.675), 2)` is `2.68`, where Python's `round(2.675, 2)` is `2.67`). On the 1,000 parity cases and a 20,000-case sweep from the generator's sampler (seed 20261103), every `pet` equals upstream's.
- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `_pet_steady_vectorised`: the zero-divisor checks test the divisor upstream divides by, except where a negative `weight` or `height` makes upstream's value complex and the library's NaN: `a_dubois`'s zero is also tested on `height`, and `r1`'s on `f_a_cl`, the factors that make the complex value zero (deviation pet_steady-4). Upstream's other divisions have a numpy scalar operand, the solved temperatures or `np.log`'s result, so they give inf or NaN, as JavaScript's do, and are not checked.
- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `pet_steady`: the docstring's citations `[Hoppe1999]_` and `[Walther2018]_` link to `docs/references.md`, and its `:py:class:` reference is `{@link PETSteady}` (ADR 0025). The rest is kept verbatim, "Wind speed" for `v` and the `wme` entry's "[met]" included, though `h = he * (1.0 - wme)` (`models/pet_steady.py:302`) reads `wme` as a fraction; so are the nested functions' docstrings and the code comments other than rows pet_steady-8 to pet_steady-10, the repeated "Calculation of the equivalent thermal resistance of body tissues" (`models/pet_steady.py:347, :368`) included.
- `src/models/pet_steady.ts` `PET_STEADY_INFO.inputs.position`: `position` and `sex` are `BaseInputs`' `choice_field`s (`classes_input.py:71-73, :80`), which `BASE_INPUTS_POSITION` and `BASE_INPUTS_SEX` (`src/_internal/define.ts`) hold once and the entries reference with the model's defaults, 'sitting' and 'male'. `BaseInputs` skips either when it is `None` (`classes_input.py:138-139`), and the parse passes `null` through. The check is case-insensitive, but the kernel compares `position == Postures.standing.value`, `position == "standing, forced convection"` and `sex == Sex.male.value` as given (`models/pet_steady.py:297, :315, :329, :331`), so 'Standing', 'STANDING, FORCED CONVECTION' and `null` compute as sitting, and 'Male', 'FEMALE' and `null` as female, in both (measured on 4.7.0).
